From card transactions to personal upgrade offers

A bank holds millions of card transactions and knows little about what each customer would value. This proof of concept turns raw transactions into one personal, compliant message about a premium card benefit that is worth real euros to that customer, and it stays silent when an offer would do harm.

Arthur Morvan
November 2026
Executive summary

The full chain works end to end, and five management questions now have an evidenced answer

Can we read spend at scale?
24.4 million transactions sorted into 14 spend sectors
Do we know who we are talking to?
2,000 customers profiled on 11 behaviours and grouped into 3 segments
Do we know when to stay silent?
425 customers (21%) are held back because an offer would backfire
Is the offer worth real money?
Every offer is priced in euros per month from the customer's own spend, net of the annual fee
Is every message safe to send?
25 of 25 messages passed rule checks and an independent review at the first attempt

Scope of the evidence. The transaction data is synthetic, and campaign response, personas and offer acceptance are simulated. The results prove that the machinery works, they do not yet measure market lift.

1

The approach

Five questions are answered in order, and each stage has one job and one output that can be audited or replaced

1. Understand the spend
Every transaction is mapped to one of 14 spend sectors, from groceries and fuel to air travel and hotels.
Why this choice
Text classifiers were benchmarked first. A simple linear model matched a fine tuned transformer at 0.99 macro F1. Where a merchant code exists it is used, as it is exact and auditable.
2. Understand the customer
One row per customer captures spend rhythm, sector mix and credit health. Customers are then grouped into 3 segments.
Why this choice
The number of segments is set by the data, and each one is named by a plain rule so that business teams can read it without a data scientist.
3. Decide whether and what
An uplift model screens out customers an offer would hurt. Each benefit is priced in euros per month, and a learning algorithm selects one offer.
Why this choice
Value comes before wording. The decision rests on money the customer already spends, and no contact is wasted on someone who should not be approached.
4. Personalise
A persona records the preferred channel, tone, motivation and language of each customer and shapes the wording.
Why this choice
Two customers with the same spend read the same message differently. Keeping the persona apart from the offer lets real preference data replace it later.
5. Write and control
An AI writer drafts the message. Rule checks and an independent AI reviewer must both pass before anything is sent.
Why this choice
A failed draft is retried twice and then passed to a person. A weak message is never sent, which protects the brand and the customer.
What comes out. One offer, one channel and one checked message per customer, or a deliberate decision not to contact.

3

The message

Every message is written from verified facts and must pass two independent controls before it leaves the bank

Inputs
Fact sheet. The only numbers the writer may quote, such as the monthly value of the benefit and the net gain after the fee.
Persona. Channel, tone, motivation, digital comfort and language.
AI writer
A language model drafts one short message under the name Claire, the assistant of the bank.
It states the value of the benefit first and never promises approval or guaranteed returns.
Control 1, rules
Five automatic checks
• Amounts shown in € only
• Greeting in the right language
• Signed by Claire
• Every number found in the fact sheet
• Length within the channel limit
Control 2, reviewer
A second, independent model scores the draft from 1 to 5 on seven criteria and needs at least 4 on each.
Tone, motivation, channel fit, language, clarity, no overpromise, factual accuracy.
Outcome
Pass. The message is approved for the preferred channel of the customer.
Fail. The writer retries twice with the feedback, then the case is flagged for a person.

5

Scope and next steps

The machinery is proven, and the next step is a live pilot that replaces simulated inputs with real ones

What is proven today
The chain runs end to end on 24.4 million transactions and 2,000 customers
Guardrails work at both ends, on who is contacted and on what is sent
Each stage is tested on its own and can be swapped, for example for a vendor service, without touching the rest
What is still simulated
Transactions come from a public synthetic data set, not from real customers
Campaign response and offer acceptance, as no campaign history exists in the data
Personas and the benefit catalogue. The interfaces accept real logs unchanged
Three improvements to raise relevance beyond the pilot
1
Visual content
Add an image per benefit and persona to emails and in app messages, drawn from a vetted, brand safe library. Success is measured by open and click uplift against text only.
2
Aspirations, not only value
Add data sources that reveal what a customer aspires to, for example an interest in wellbeing that points to a premium wellbeing offer. Consent and data protection review come first.
3
Tone from real interactions
Read tone, register and language from past exchanges between the customer and the bank, replacing the synthetic persona. Applied only to customers whose spend justifies a new card.
Recommended next step. Run a controlled pilot on a consented customer sample with a holdout group, so that real response data replaces the simulated campaign and market lift can be measured.
